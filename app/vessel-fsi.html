How it works

Blood flow follows the one-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations along centrelines traced from the surface. Each cross-section obeys the inflation of a thick-walled, incompressible tube by default with a two-layer Holzapfel–Gasser–Ogden wall (media and adventitia, each with two collagen fibre families that carry load only when stretched, optional fibre dispersion and opening angle for residual stress), or with a 5-parameter Mooney–Rivlin wall; either is solved exactly by radial equilibrium. Pressure and wall area are solved together at every time step, so the wall changes the flow and the flow moves the wall.

The scanned shape is taken to be the vessel at the pressure entered under “Pressure when scanned”; the unloaded size is worked out from it. Outlets connect to a three-element Windkessel whose resistance and compliance are adjusted to reach the blood pressure entered.

Limits: flow is averaged over each cross-section, so local recirculation and wall shear detail come from ForgeFlow’s 3D solver; the wall is passive (no active muscle tone) and purely elastic (no viscous damping); veins near collapse are outside this model. The wall presets are calibrated fits, listed with their accuracy; for a specific patient or tissue, enter coefficients fitted to inflation or biaxial test data.

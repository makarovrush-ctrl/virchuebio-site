Privacy policy Effective 18 November 2026

Privacy policy.

How the VirchueBio team handles information when you visit www.virchuebio.com and use the platform, including work you choose to submit to us.

In short

  • Using the platform: your files and results are processed in your browser and are not sent to us.
  • Submitting is your choice: we receive your work only when you press Submit to VirchueBio and confirm. Save my work only saves a file on your computer.
  • No identity: we do not ask for your name or email, and submissions contain no file names, DICOM images or DICOM tags.
  • Patient scans: a vessel from a patient or animal scan can be submitted only after you confirm it is de-identified and that you may share it.
  • Deletion: send us the case ID shown after you submit and we delete the case within 30 days. Otherwise submissions are kept for up to 7 years.
  • We never sell information, show advertising or use tracking cookies.

Visit statistics

Since 17 November 2026 we count visits to the website and the platform with GoatCounter (goatcounter.com), an open-source analytics service hosted in Germany and Finland. It uses no cookies and stores nothing in your browser. It records the page visited, the referring website, browser and operating system, screen size, language and your country, which it works out from your IP address. It does not store your IP address or identify you, and it shares nothing with third parties. In the platform we also count actions, such as starting a run, calculating the thrombosis number Θ or running the demo; these counts contain no geometry, settings or results. We see totals only.

Who we are

The platform is operated by the VirchueBio team: Dr Mackenzie Siyuan Zhuang, Allan Sun and Professor Ken-Tye Yong ("we", "us"). Contact: siyuan.zhuang@sydney.edu.au, allan.sun@sydney.edu.au or ken.yong@sydney.edu.au. We handle personal information in line with the Australian Privacy Principles.

What we collect

When you visit the website. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which receives standard request information such as your IP address and browser type.

When you use the platform. Your files are opened and calculated in your web browser on your own computer. Nothing is sent to us unless you submit it. Exports and files saved with Save my work stay on your computer. DICOM scans opened in DICOM vessel extraction are read in your browser and never uploaded; only the vessel surface made from a scan can be submitted, and it carries no DICOM images or tags.

When you submit your work. When you press Submit to VirchueBio in Experimental validation or Feedback and confirm, we receive and store: the vessel geometry (surface triangles, with the file header removed); your settings (units, openings, flow, fluid, mesh and model inputs); the result summary; wall shear or thrombosis number Θ on each wall face; the report you submitted (your clot markings with the flow at each marked point, the outcome and the details you entered, or your ratings, comments and clot sites); the terms version you agreed to and when; your answer about where the geometry came from; and a random case ID. Before anything is sent, the platform removes file names and file paths. We do not receive DICOM images or tags (only the vessel surface built from them), and we do not ask for your name, email address or any clinical details. You must not include them.

Technical records. Our upload service on Google Cloud keeps your IP address in memory only, to apply fair-use limits. Google Cloud's request logs record the time and IP address of each upload; they are kept for 30 days for security and are not stored with the cases.

When you post in the forum or email us. Forum posts are public and handled by GitHub under its own privacy statement. If you email us, we keep your address and message to reply.

How we use it

  • to research thrombosis and blood flow, and to develop, test and improve the platform and its models using the work you submit;
  • to run the platform and protect it from misuse;
  • to answer your questions and requests, including deletion requests.

We publish findings drawn from many cases only, never a submitted case on its own.

Where it is stored and who can see it

Submitted cases are stored encrypted in Google Cloud's Sydney region, in Australia. The upload service can add files but cannot read, change or delete them. Only named members of the VirchueBio research team can open them, and access is logged. We use service providers (Google Cloud for storage, GitHub for the website and forum) under their own terms. GitHub is based in the United States. We do not otherwise disclose your information unless the law requires it.

How long we keep it

  • Submitted cases: up to 7 years from submission, then deleted, or within 30 days of a deletion request.
  • Upload request logs: 30 days.
  • Emails: as long as needed to deal with your request.

Your choices and rights

You can use the platform without submitting anything. To have a submitted case deleted, email us its case ID; we delete it within 30 days, although findings already published from many cases cannot be withdrawn. Because we do not collect names or email addresses with submissions, we can find a case only by its ID. You can ask what information we hold about you. If you have a concern, contact us first; if you are not satisfied, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (oaic.gov.au).

Other points

The platform is intended for people aged 18 or over. If this policy changes, we will update the effective date above, and the Submit dialog will ask you to agree to the current version; changes do not apply to work submitted earlier unless you agree.
